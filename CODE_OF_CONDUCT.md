# Code of conduct

## Why this exists

AI Chat is a small project run by one person. This document says what is expected of
people taking part, what is not acceptable, and what happens when someone reports a
problem. It applies to everyone equally, including the maintainer.

## Scope

This applies in every space this project uses: issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages, code review and the content of contributions themselves. It also
applies when someone is representing the project elsewhere — for example, posting as a
maintainer or contributor of AI Chat on another platform.

It does not give the project authority over unrelated conduct in someone's private life
or in other communities. What it does cover is conduct that makes this project's spaces
worse for the people in them.

## What is expected

- Assume the other person is acting in good faith and has less context than you do.
- Criticise the change, not the person who wrote it. "This drops the permission check"
  is review; "you clearly do not understand permissions" is not.
- Accept that the maintainer says no to things, often, and sometimes without a long
  explanation. Disagreement is fine; pressure campaigns are not.
- Be honest about what you tested and what you did not. Overstated claims waste the
  reviewer's time and undermine trust.
- Respect people's stated names and pronouns.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, whether public or in private messages: personal attacks, insults,
  sustained disruption, or following someone across threads to continue an argument.
- Demeaning or exclusionary comments about a person or group — including about race,
  ethnicity, nationality, gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation,
  disability, neurotype, age, body size, appearance, religion or caste.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual or romantic attention.
- Publishing someone's private information — address, workplace, personal accounts,
  private correspondence — without their explicit permission.
- Threats of any kind, including threats to reputation or livelihood.
- Deliberately submitting malicious code, a misleading commit message, or a contribution
  whose described behaviour is not its actual behaviour.
- Using the project's spaces to advertise, recruit or spam.

Being technically correct does not excuse any of the above.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately. Do not open a public issue about someone's conduct; it makes
things worse for the person reporting and for everyone reading.

> **Private conduct contact:** [mcgloneb@gmail.com](mailto:mcgloneb@gmail.com)

Reports go to Bill ([@mcgloneb](https://github.com/mcgloneb)), who is the only
moderator. There is no second moderator and no appeals panel. If your report is about
Bill, you may reasonably prefer not to use this channel; GitHub's own
[abuse reporting](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) is independent of this project
and remains available to you.

Please include what happened, where, roughly when, and links if the exchange is public.
You do not need to have a perfect record — report it anyway.

What happens next: Bill acknowledges the report, looks at the material, and decides.
Reports are handled privately. Your identity is not shared with the person reported
without your agreement, though be aware that in a project this small the context can
make it obvious. Handling is best-effort, like everything else here; there is no
guaranteed response time.

Conduct reports are not support requests and not security reports. See
[SUPPORT.md](SUPPORT.md) and [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) for those.

## What can happen

Bill decides the response, in proportion to what happened and whether it is repeated:

1. **A private word.** An explanation of what was wrong and what to do instead.
2. **A warning.** The same, recorded, with a clear statement that a repeat leads to a
   ban.
3. **A temporary ban** from interacting in the project's spaces.
4. **A permanent ban.**

Separately, and at any time, content that breaches this document may be edited, hidden
or deleted, and a pull request may be closed. A single serious incident — a threat,
doxxing, deliberately malicious code — can go straight to a permanent ban.

## Good faith

Reporting in good faith, even about something that turns out not to be a breach, is
never itself a breach. Reporting in bad faith — to harass someone or to win a technical
argument — is.
